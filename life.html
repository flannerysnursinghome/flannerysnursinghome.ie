Life here

No two days are the same, and nobody is on a timetable they did not agree to.

The ordinary rhythm of a house, not an institution.

Mornings are slow on purpose. The paper, tea, the radio, and a word with whoever is about. Afternoons tend to fill up: music, a bit of baking, cards, or a walk around the garden if the weather allows.

Residents keep their own routines and their own things. Rooms are furnished with photographs, cushions and throws from home, and the day rooms end up looking like a large family living room, which is what they are.

Families come and go as they please and are welcome to join in: a birthday, a sing-song, or a quiet hour with the paper.

Something for everyone

Our Activities Coordinator plans the week and organises outings and events. Nothing is compulsory, and everything can be joined at your own pace.

  • Arts and crafts

    Painting, knitting and card-making. Something to make, and something to keep.

  • Music and singing

    Sessions, sing-songs and visiting musicians.

  • Gentle exercise

    Chair-based movement and walks in the garden, at whatever pace suits.

  • Social events

    Birthdays, bingo, quizzes, and the weekly residents' baking.

  • Reminiscence

    Pictures and films of life in the days of old, and the conversation that follows.

  • Spiritual care

    Mass celebrated weekly in the house, and residents of other faiths supported in their own practice.

Spaces that feel like home

Rooms are furnished with people's own things. These are the shared rooms.

Food, movement, and Mass

Food

Our chefs cook fresh each day: an extensive menu displayed daily, good home cooking and home baking, and special or medically advised diets catered for. Meals can be taken in either dining room, including the assisted dining room where staff help at the table, or in your own room.

Mealtimes are protected. Staff interruptions are kept to a minimum, so people can eat without being rushed or disturbed.

Physiotherapy

A chartered physiotherapist visits every week, working with residents who are recovering or who want to keep moving as well as they can.

Mass

Roman Catholic Mass is celebrated weekly in the house. Residents of other faiths are supported in their own practice, and we will help arrange a visit from a minister of any denomination.

Come and see a day for yourself.

Mornings are our quietest time, if you would like to see the house as it really is rather than on show. Ring and we will arrange a time.
